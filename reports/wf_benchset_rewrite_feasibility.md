# Can the spec rewrite be tested on benchset v1? No — and the reason matters

Status: **not runnable, and this supersedes a recommendation I made in three artefacts.**
[`wf_optimised_gain_source.md`](wf_optimised_gain_source.md) §5, the §6 cell of
`notebooks/experiments/wf_optimised_usecase_baseline.ipynb`, and `CONTEXT.md` §6b all say the
n=6 result should be confirmed by porting the rewrite to the 28 `benchset_v1` briefs. **It cannot
be**, for two independent reasons, either of which is on its own sufficient. Both were found by
attempting it.

🟢 clears the 0.03 noise floor · 🟡 real but under it · ⚪ engineering finding

## 0. What was being tested, and what "runnable" would have required

The n=6 finding: rewriting a use-case objective from material the model never reads is worth
**+0.014** mean zero-label ROC-AUC, and *which* specs benefit is ordered by
`scripts/spec_linter.py` findings at rho **+0.65** — while being unrelated to how well the use case
was already scoring (rho −0.03). That is six specs from one team, so it needs a second surface.

A usable second surface needs three things:

1. **Variance in the predictor.** If every spec scores the same on the linter, the linter cannot
   order anything, and rho is undefined.
2. **A leak-free reservoir.** The rewrite's entire validity rests on moving text the analyst
   already wrote in a field *no feature reads*, and never consulting a paper or a label. Without
   that, any gain is manufactured (`DATA_BRIEF.md` honest-limit #2).
3. Paper vectors and labels, which benchset v1 does have.

Benchset v1 fails (1) and (2).

## 1. 🔴 Blocker one — the predictor has no variance

`python scripts/spec_linter.py --specs benchset` over all 28:

**0 findings. 28 of 28 specs clean.**

That is not a bug — it is the same 0/28 the linter's own calibration records, and it is *why* the
false-positive rate is meaningful. But it means the measure that carried the n=6 result is
**constant** here. Every other measure is near-constant too:

| measure | benchset v1 (n=28) | TIRI (n=6) | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `linter_findings` | **0 – 0**, sd **0.00**, 1 distinct value | 0 – 2, sd 0.75, 3 distinct | 🔴 no variance at all |
| `n_terms` | 12 – 15, sd **0.61**, 4 distinct | 3 – 15, sd **4.29**, 6 distinct | 🔴 7× less spread |
| `checkability` | 0.679 – 0.906, sd 0.054 | 0.500 – 1.000, sd **0.166** | 🟡 3× less, and a rejected instrument |
| `objective_words` | 20 – 185, sd 46.5 | 11 – 53, sd 14.6 | ⚪ real spread — but this is the one measure that **failed** on TIRI (rho −0.20) |

**The 28 benchset briefs are near-identical in every way that mattered.** They were generated by one
automated process from one kind of source, so they are uniform by construction: all 28 carry
populated term lists (12–15 terms), populated `domain_industry` *and* `domain_application`, and an
objective that is the review's abstract. TIRI's six are hand-written by two people and vary from a
3-term spec with an 11-word objective to a 15-term spec with a 53-word one.

*ELI18: to ask "do badly-written briefs benefit most from a rewrite?" you need some briefs that are
badly written and some that are not. These 28 are all written the same way, to the same standard, by
the same machine. There is nothing to compare.*

⚪ **Two engineering fixes fell out of running this**, both committed:

- `--specs benchset` **crashed** (`IndexError`) whenever nothing fired, because the report writer
  assumed at least one finding. A linter that cannot report a clean run is broken for its happiest
  case.
- `--specs benchset` **silently overwrote the shipped `wf_spec_linter.md`** with a 28-row subset
  reading "0 findings" — true, and deeply misleading beside the all-34 report it replaced. Narrower
  runs now write to `wf_spec_linter_<scope>.md`; `--specs all` keeps the canonical name.

## 2. 🔴 Blocker two — there is no leak-free reservoir, and the obvious substitute is label-derived

`scripts/optimise_usecases.py` moves text out of `performance_criteria`, `constraints`,
`decision_criteria` and `notes`. Across all 28 benchset `*.usecase.json`:

| reservoir field | populated |
|---|---|
| `performance_criteria` | **0 / 28** |
| `decision_criteria` (any of four sub-lists) | **0 / 28** |
| `constraints` (trl / scale / cost) | **0 / 28** |
| `notes` | 28 / 28 — but all provenance boilerplate about the benchmark, median 42 words, describing *how the corpus was built*, not the subject |

**So the rewrite is a literal no-op on all 28.** There is nothing to move.

The tempting substitute is the richer `*.spec.json`, which carries `population`, `interventions`,
`outcomes` and `evidence.include_types` populated on 28/28 — the review's eligibility framework, and
read by no feature. **It is disqualified**, by its own metadata:

```
"drafted_from": "review_abstract+labels"      ← all 28
```

Those fields were drafted **from the labels**. And the same files carry two more that make the
problem concrete rather than theoretical:

- `exemplars` — 28/28, median 8 each — literally **the titles of accepted papers** ("typical
  accepted paper, nearest the positive centroid");
- `near_misses` — 28/28 — descriptions of **rejected** papers and why they were rejected.

Moving any of that into the objective would be writing the answer key into the question. It is the
precise failure `optimise_usecases.py` was designed around, and it would produce a large, entirely
fake gain.

🟡 **Note the asymmetry that makes this benchset-specific.** The benchset `objective` *already* is
the review abstract, so leakage is baked into the baseline too — which is `DATA_BRIEF.md`
honest-limit #2 and is fine for *comparative* work where both arms carry it equally. It is not fine
here, because the whole point is measuring what an *increment* of brief material buys, and on this
surface every available increment is label-derived.

## 3. What this does and does not change

**Unchanged.** Every measured number in
[`wf_optimised_usecase_baseline.md`](wf_optimised_usecase_baseline.md) and
[`wf_optimised_gain_source.md`](wf_optimised_gain_source.md) stands. Nothing here is evidence against
them.

**Changed.** The confirmation I said they needed is **not available on this data**, so the n=6
caveat is not a temporary one to be discharged next week — it is the ceiling. Specifically:

- 🟡 *"`linter_findings` orders where a rewrite pays (rho +0.65)"* stays **provisional
  indefinitely**. Use it as a hint about where to spend an hour of an analyst's time; do not build
  anything that depends on it.
- The depth-profile finding — that the whole gain sits at 50–75% read depth with nothing in the top
  20% — is **not affected by any of this**, because it needs no second surface. It is a statement
  about where in a ranking the effect lands, measured on the six specs where the effect exists. That
  remains the finding most likely to change what anyone does.

## 4. What would actually work

| option | verdict |
|---|---|
| **More analyst-written specs** from real users of `academic_agent` | 🟢 The only clean route. The variance the test needs is a property of *humans writing specs*, and only the labelling tool produces those. Even 6 more would double n |
| Degrade the 28 uniform benchset specs to create variance, then test whether linter findings predict the cost | 🔴 **Circular.** `spec_linter.py` imports `FLUFF`, `VAGUE` and `GENERIC` *from the ablation module* — it would detect the damage by construction, because those are the same strings |
| Use `checkability` as the predictor on benchset instead | 🔴 It has a third of TIRI's spread, and it already failed its own pre-registered bar (pooled IQR 0.055 against 0.2, `wf_checkability_audit.md`). Reviving a rejected instrument on a surface where it is weaker is not a confirmation |
| Hand-write 28 spec variants of differing quality | 🟡 Possible, but the quality ordering would be my judgement, and the thing under test is whether a *mechanical* measure captures spec quality. Circular in a subtler way |

**Recommendation: do not spend more on confirming this one.** Record the n=6 result as provisional,
keep the depth-profile finding (which needs no confirmation), and let n grow naturally as the
labelling tool produces real specs.
